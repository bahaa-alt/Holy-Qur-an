What is characteristic of this part of the Qur'an?

Pick a scope — a surah, a juz', the Meccan or Medinan corpus, a window of the revelation order — and every root is measured against the rest of the Qur'an. A count on its own cannot answer a research question, because the question is always “compared to what”.

How to read this

The unit is a rooted occurrence — a segment carrying a root, this app's definition of an occurrence throughout. Rates are per 10,000 rooted occurrences, computed on the same basis inside the scope and outside it. Particles and pronouns carry no root and are not counted on either side.

G² is log-likelihood (Dunning 1993): how surprising the difference is, given how much text is involved. It grows with the size of the corpus, so a large G² on a tiny difference is real but may be uninteresting.

Log ratio (Hardie 2014) is the size of the difference, in doublings: +1 means twice as common here, +3 means eight times. It does not grow with corpus size, and it is unstable on small counts — which is what the minimum-occurrence floor is for. The smaller range underneath is a 95% confidence interval on that log ratio itself, via the delta-method standard error for a log relative risk (Katz, Baptista, Azen & Pike 1978) — the same method behind the risk-ratio interval in a meta-analysis forest plot — so a reader can see whether two effect sizes are actually distinguishable, not just compare their single best estimates.

The smaller range under each rate is a 95% Wilson score confidence interval (Wilson 1927): where the true rate plausibly lies, not just its single best estimate. Preferred here over the textbook normal approximation because it stays inside 0-100% and keeps its stated coverage even at the small counts a keyness table often runs on.

Bonferroni and FDR (Benjamini-Hochberg, 1995) answer different questions and are offered as alternatives, not as one right answer. Bonferroni bounds the chance of any false positive at all, across every root that occurs anywhere in the comparison — strict, and conservative enough to bury real, moderate effects among 1,651 simultaneous tests. FDR instead bounds the expected proportion of false positives among the rows it calls significant, computed only among the roots above the minimum-occurrence floor, and has more power to find those effects at some known cost in false discoveries.

DP is Gries's deviation of proportions (2008): how far a root's distribution across the 114 surahs departs from what those surahs' sizes would predict. It separates a word used 300 times across eighty surahs from one used 300 times in a single passage — two facts this app previously reported identically.

DP alone is descriptive, not a significance test: a root occurring only a handful of times can look concentrated by pure luck. “Test significance” runs a permutation (Monte Carlo) test for that one root: simulate its occurrences landing by chance, in proportion to each surah's size, many times over, and see how often that alone produces a DP as high as observed. A small p-value means the concentration is unlikely to be a coincidence of where a rare word happened to land.

The reference corpus is always the rest of the Qur'an, which is a small corpus by the standards of these measures: read a single row as a lead to follow, not a result to publish. Roots are the unit here; lemmas, grammatical features and collocation follow.
